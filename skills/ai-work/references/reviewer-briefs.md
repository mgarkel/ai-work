# Reviewer briefs

Give each reviewer its brief, filled in, plus the shared rules at the end. Fill in the bracketed parts. Each brief starts by saying exactly what the reviewer was given.

## Plan reviewers

Each gets: the plan file (`plan.md`), the sources the plan names, and its brief. Nothing from the conversation.

**Devil's advocate**

> You've been given a plan for [job] and its sources, and nothing else. Challenge it. What assumptions does it rest on, and which are weakest? Would meeting every outcome in it actually achieve the objectives, or could they all be met while the real problem remains? Is this the right problem to solve? Give the strongest case against the chosen approach. If you can't find a good one, say so: that's useful too.

**Scope guardian**

> You've been given a plan for [job] and its sources, and nothing else. Find the smallest plan that still meets its outcomes. Flag steps, extras, or polish that the outcomes don't need, and anything that quietly adds work nobody asked for. Also flag anything the outcomes need that the plan leaves out.

**Consistency check**

> You've been given a plan for [job], and nothing else. Check that it hangs together: every step serves an outcome, every outcome has steps, decisions in one section don't contradict another, the checklist is covered, and the stop points are clear. Flag anything a person carrying out the plan would read two ways.

**Fact check**

> You've been given a plan for [job] and the sources it names, and nothing else. List the facts, numbers, and claims the plan relies on. For each, check it against the sources: confirmed (say where), wrong (say what the source says), or can't be checked from what you have. Don't trust the plan's own word for anything.

**Audience lens**

> You've been given a plan for [job] and its sources, and nothing else. The work is for [audience]. How will they react to what this plan will produce? What will they need that it doesn't give them, what could land badly, and who else is affected that the plan doesn't consider?

## Work reviewers

**Reader test**

First, write five to ten questions the real audience would need the work to answer (for example, for an investor update: "How did the fund perform this quarter?", "What do you need from me?"). Then give the reader only the finished work and the questions.

> You've been given a piece of work and some questions, and nothing else. You are [audience]. Answer each question using only the work. For each: your answer, and anything unclear or ambiguous. Then say: what does the work assume you already know? Are there any contradictions?

Compare the answers with what the work was meant to say. Wrong answers and confusion are findings. The work passes when the reader answers correctly and finds no new gaps.

**Checklist and done check**

> You've been given a piece of work, the plan's north star and definition of done, and the job's checklist, and nothing else. Go line by line. For each outcome, each part of the definition of done, and each checklist item: met, partly met, or not met, and where in the work.

**Fact and source check**

> You've been given a piece of work and the sources it cites, and nothing else. Check every fact, number, and quote: does it have a source, and does the source actually say that? Redo any calculations. Flag anything without a source, anything the source doesn't support, and any number that's wrong.

**Audience and sensitivity**

> You've been given a piece of work and a description of its audience ([audience]), and nothing else. Read it as they will. Flag anything that could land badly (tone, wording, what's said or left out), anything sensitive, and any personal information that shouldn't be there: health, family, money, private matters, or identifying numbers.

**Devil's advocate** (only when the work recommends or decides something)

> You've been given a piece of work that recommends [recommendation], and its sources, and nothing else. Make the strongest case against the recommendation. What would a smart skeptic say? What evidence would change the conclusion? If you can't find a good counterargument, say so.

## Shared rules for every reviewer

Add these to every brief:

> Only read; don't change any files. Report only what you're fairly sure of, and no nitpicks. For each finding: quote the passage (a few words), say what would go wrong for the reader or the job, rate it must fix, should fix, or nice to fix, and suggest a fix if it's obvious. List concerns you couldn't verify separately, under "Worth knowing". End with one or two things that work well. If you were given less than this brief says, say what's missing.
